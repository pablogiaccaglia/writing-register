# Documentation and READMEs

{#documentation.results-first refines=order.point-first} For results, open with the short version: what works, the two or three numbers that show it, what they mean, and how to proceed. The method and its mathematics follow in full, before the detailed results. {#documentation.navigable} A long document is easy to navigate, with clear sections and a table of contents once it runs long. {#documentation.paths} Say what works now and how to use it, and keep getting started and everyday usage as separate paths. {#documentation.research-order} A research section runs from the question to the data, to what was measured, to how to read the figure and what it means; a figure caption says what its axes are. {#documentation.no-attribution refines=content.no-trivia} Documentation does not attribute statements to people ("the lead said", "a colleague found"); it states the content. {#documentation.no-emoji} Emoji never appear in headings. {#documentation.one-place refines=references.pointer-adds} Each kind of content, such as the next steps or a known limitation, has one named place in the documentation, and other texts point to it instead of restating it. {#documentation.results-overview} A research report that closes a body of work has one overview of every experiment and approach tried, side by side, each with its result and the size of the data it used. {#documentation.handoff} A hand-off is self-contained: it explains each delegated item with the reasoning behind it, and tells the recipient what to read, in what order, and what to take from each item.
