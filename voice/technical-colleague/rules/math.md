# Mathematics and notation

- {#math.symbol-at-first-use refines=introduce.first-use} When a text uses notation, every symbol, index and named set is defined in words in the sentence where it first enters a formula, including what kind of object it is. A definition given earlier in prose does not excuse defining it again where the mathematics uses it.
- {#math.renders-where-read} When a text uses notation, every formula and symbol is written in a form the place where it will be read can render, and is checked there, because a sentence whose symbol does not appear cannot be followed.
- {#math.one-meaning refines=register.one-term} When a text uses notation, each symbol names one quantity from start to finish. Never reuse a letter for a second quantity or operator, and never give a new letter to something that already has one.
- {#math.plain-reading} When a text defines a quantity, a plain sentence follows the formula saying what the quantity means in the problem at hand, what a large or a small value tells the reader, and why it is used.
- {#math.geometric-reading} When a quantity has a simple geometric meaning, such as the length of a vector or the angle between two vectors, say so in words beside its definition and translate that reading back into what it means for the thing being measured.
- {#math.purpose-formula-derivation} When a text presents a method, a step or a quantity, it first says what problem it solves and why it is needed, then gives the formula, then the derivation behind it.
- {#math.derivation-in-moves} When a formula is presented as the answer to a problem, or an equation or identity is stated as holding, derive it on the page in visible steps, one operation per step, each with the reason it holds, including the property that makes it valid. A line that packs several operations together is split.
- {#math.inputs-outputs} When a text describes a method, it says what goes in, what comes out and how each input is built from the raw data. After an operator is defined, say in plain words what it maps from and to, and which form of a quantity each step uses.
- {#math.conditions refines=claims.provenance} When a text reports a measured value or a formula, it states the conditions it holds under: the value of any parameter it depends on, the value actually set for a tunable parameter, and why a correction is applied to one side of a comparison and not the other.
- {#math.scale-reference} When a text shows a quantity on a scale, it says what the scale is referenced to and why, including whether a figure's colour scale is set per panel or shared, and explains how a value outside the range the reader expects can arise.
- {#math.what-a-score-cannot-show} When a score can reach its best value without a perfect match, or behaves in a surprising way, say what it cannot show and pair it with the measure that exposes the error it hides.
- {#math.worked-example-first} When a statement is counter-intuitive, or a definition is about to be adopted, back it with a small worked example, a few numbers or a handful of cases, before asking the reader to accept it.
- {#math.theory-where-it-clarifies} A document that reports or proposes a method explains the theory and mathematics behind it wherever they make the method or a conclusion clearer, and each conclusion carries the reasoning that supports it. Mathematics added only for show is left out.
